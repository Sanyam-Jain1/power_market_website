Day-ahead or real-time?

A battery can sell a day early, or wait for the real-time market. On four and a half years of IEX prices, waiting on its own earns less. Waiting to correct the day-ahead plan earns more, in every year and every month of the year.

Every day a battery owner on IEX makes the same choice. Bid into the day-ahead market by noon the day before, at prices that clear around 13:00. Or wait for the real-time market, which clears an hour ahead of each half-hour of delivery. Waiting means more information. Does it mean more money?

We tested every option on each delivery day from 1 May 2022 to 31 October 2026: 1,645 days of IEX prices, block by block. The battery is 100 MW / 200 MWh, 87% round trip, kept between 10% and 90% charge, one cycle a day, paying ₹20/MWh exchange fees on each side and a ₹300 wear charge on every MWh discharged. No strategy sees a price before it was published.

Waiting on its own earns less

Planned on the last 7 days' prices, the battery earned ₹32.7 lakh per MW-year on day-ahead and ₹27.5 lakh on real-time. Even with perfect hindsight, real-time pays less: ₹32.1 lakh against ₹34.2 lakh.

The reason is when the two markets differ. Over the last 12 months, real-time cleared on average ₹800–1,100/MWh below day-ahead between 18:00 and 22:00, when a battery sells. It cleared ₹70–330 above day-ahead in the solar hours, when a battery buys. A battery that only uses real-time sells cheaper and buys dearer.

Waiting to correct pays

The average hides large swings. In a typical block the two markets end ₹500–1,500/MWh apart, one way or the other. That is room to trade, if the battery knows roughly where real-time will land.

So we tested a battery that does both. At noon it commits its day-ahead plan, made on the last 7 days' prices. At 22:00 the next day's day-ahead prices are public. It re-plans its schedule on a forecast of real-time, the average of those prices and last week's real-time prices, and buys or sells the difference from its day-ahead position in real-time.

It earned ₹33.9 lakh per MW-year, ₹1.2 lakh more than day-ahead alone. Over the last 12 months it earned ₹38.8 lakh against ₹37.0 lakh, more than even the hindsight best on day-ahead alone (₹38.4 lakh).

It beat day-ahead alone in every year: by ₹1.5 lakh in 2022, ₹1.0 lakh in 2023 and 2024, ₹0.6 lakh in 2025 and ₹2.0 lakh in 2026 so far. It also beat it in every calendar month, at a yearly rate of ₹0.3 lakh in June up to ₹3.6 lakh in October. Day by day it won 56% of the time and lost 43%: this is an edge, not a sure thing.

The gain holds for other batteries. For a 4-hour battery it is ₹1.9 lakh (₹58.6 to ₹60.5 lakh). With two cycles a day it is ₹1.5 lakh (₹39.5 to ₹41.1 lakh). Under a rule that allows charging only from 10:00 to 15:00 it is ₹0.8 lakh.

The forecast is the whole trick

We tried three forecasts of real-time for the 22:00 re-plan. On the next day's day-ahead prices alone, the battery earned ₹31.7 lakh, less than not correcting at all. Adding the recent real-time-minus-day-ahead gap made it worse, at ₹31.0 lakh. Only the blend of day-ahead prices and last week's real-time prices paid. Each correction pays a second exchange fee, so a forecast that is slightly wrong loses money.

Because we picked the best of three, treat the ₹1.2 lakh as an estimate to be checked on new data, not a promise. That it held in every year and every month is encouraging, not proof.

A simpler idea does nothing. Choosing at noon the day before which market to buy and sell each block in, from last week's averages, earned ₹32.7 lakh: the same as day-ahead alone.

What it means

  • Don't move to real-time; add it. A battery that only trades real-time gives up about ₹5 lakh per MW a year. One that keeps its day-ahead plan and corrects it in real-time gains about ₹1–2 lakh.
  • The correction is cheap to run. It needs one re-plan a night, after day-ahead results are out, and no data a battery owner does not already have.
  • Most of the prize is still there. With hindsight in both markets, a 2-hour battery could have earned ₹38.2 lakh at one cycle and ₹55.8 lakh at two. The correction reaches ₹33.9 and ₹41.1 lakh. Re-planning during the day, session by session, is where the rest is.

Method: the backtester, its strategy specification and its look-ahead tests are open. The day-ahead leg settles at IEX day-ahead clearing prices and the correction at real-time clearing prices, with fees on both legs. Each day starts and ends at 10% charge. Figures are after exchange fees and wear, before transmission charges, O&M and deviation charges. Backtested results are not a guarantee of future returns.
